History, learning and opportunity

Alex Howells

My work explores the ways in which societies organise learning – and have organised it in the past – in pursuit of social, economic and political goals, from decolonisation and development to lifelong learning.

I am interested in how promises about learning travel across borders, change over time and shape people's lives, particularly in the global South. I also support international organisations, governments and partners with learning policy and practice, connecting local experience to the global governance of education.

About

Background

My career has so far been spent between the classroom, the international organisation and the archive. I began as a primary teacher in Manchester, trained in comparative education and international development in Edinburgh, and later worked at the UNESCO Institute for Lifelong Learning in Hamburg, where I coordinated policy dialogues, workshops and research across Africa and Asia. These experiences underpin my doctoral research in international history and politics at the Geneva Graduate Institute.
