The promise of education for work

Alex Howells

My work and research concern the promise of education for work, the expectation that schooling leads to a rewarding livelihood. In practice I advise ministries across Africa, Asia and Europe on lifelong learning, non-formal education and flexible pathways, the parts of that promise built for young people and adults the formal system leaves behind. In research I follow that promise through the twentieth century, from its making to its repeated remaking when the work failed to follow.

My doctoral research centres on Kenya, from late colonialism to independence. The promise there was forged across borders, by colonial officials, bilateral partners and international bodies alongside the Kenyan state, then remade whenever the work failed to follow. The same pattern runs through today's debates on youth unemployment, skills and second-chance provision.

Background

My career has so far been spent between the classroom, the international organisation and the archive: primary teaching in Manchester, comparative education and international development in Edinburgh, and policy dialogues, workshops and research on lifelong learning in Hamburg, across Africa, Asia and Europe. This practice now rests on doctoral research in the history and politics of education in Geneva.
