Work

Advisory & practice

I work on lifelong learning and non-formal education — policy and systems, flexible pathways for young people and adults let down by formal schooling, and learning cities — for ministries and partners across Africa and Asia.

Practice

What I do

I advise ministries, international organisations and institutions on lifelong learning and non-formal education: national policy and planning, the recognition, validation and accreditation of learning, provision for out-of-school children and youth, and the coordination of learning at city level. Much of the work carries local practice up into international policy and brings international commitments back down to the ministries and municipalities responsible for delivery. My doctoral research on the history and politics of education in the global South adds a longer foundation: the origins and design of formal schooling and the roots of exclusion from it.

Policy & systems

Lifelong learning policy and systems

Policy advice, planning and capacity development for ministries; national strategies, governance and financing.

Non-formal & pathways

Non-formal education and flexible pathways

Equivalency programmes, alternative provision for out-of-school children and youth and the recognition, validation and accreditation of learning.

Learning cities

Learning cities and local provision

Municipal coordination, community learning centres and the UNESCO Global Network of Learning Cities.

History & politics

History and politics of education

The promise of education in the global South, the governing of the young, transnational development interventions and the historical roots of exclusion from formal schooling.

Selected engagements

Prominent engagements

UNESCO Institute for Lifelong Learning (UIL)

  • Led capacity-building and policy advice on lifelong learning for ministries and government stakeholders, including a flagship national education systems workshop run online and in Shanghai.
  • Advised Ministries of Education in Ukraine, Türkiye and Cambodia on strengthening lifelong learning systems and produced materials for the development of learning cities.
  • Coordinated the UNESCO Global Network of Learning Cities with partners including the UNESCO Chair of Global Health Education and the German Ministry of Education and Research.

DVV International

Supported cities in Armenia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Georgia and Kosovo — across the Western Balkans and South Caucasus — in developing lifelong learning at the local level, and contributed to a regional policy workshop and publication in Southeast Asia (Cambodia, Lao PDR and Thailand).

International policy workshops

Designed and led policy dialogues bringing together policymakers from across Africa and Asia, among them Indonesia, Kenya, the Maldives, Mongolia, the Philippines, Uzbekistan and Zimbabwe.

Background

Educational background
